# Long-form (1–30 min) streamer-clip videos: what works, and why (2026-11-24)

**Study.** 46 streamer-clip channels (21 IRL / just-chatting-leaning, 22 gaming, 5 GTA RP; 38 clip channels, 10 streamers' own channels). Last ≤150 uploads each → **3,461 settled long videos** (16:9, 1–30 min, ≥7 days old). A video is an **outlier** if it got ≥3× the median views of the 30 long videos posted around it, so channel size cancels out. **460 (13.3%)** are outliers; that is the baseline every rate below is compared with. Data and every rate: `long-form-streamers-2026-11.json`.

**How it was coded.** All 460 outliers + 460 random normal videos were coded for title idea (920 titles). Thumbnails were coded for 288 of those 920 (see Limits). Apple Vision measured faces and overlay text on **all 3,461** thumbnails. 15 outliers got a close look (thumbnail, first 30 s, "most replayed", chapters).

**Gate.** A pattern is "working" only if its outlier rate is ≥2× baseline (≥26.6%), n ≥ 10, with outliers from ≥3 channels. Everything else is a lean or a note.

---

## 1. Core principles (for your review, nothing built yet)

### Passed the gate
**P1. Sell a named person's energy in bulk, where the footage is real life.** A title that names one star and promises a collection of one quality ("X FUNNIEST Moments (Compilation)", "Best Moments", "Clips That…") is the most reliable format found.
- IRL / just-chatting videos with compilation wording: **29.1% outliers (2.2×), 326 videos, 10 channels**.
- Same wording on gaming footage: 16.2% (1.2×); on GTA RP: too few (n=12).
- The coded view agrees: IRL mood compilations 33.9% (2.5×, n=113, 8 channels) vs gaming mood compilations 13.0% (1.0×).
- Why it works: the click is for a person the viewer already likes plus a guaranteed feeling. The "collection" removes the risk of a dull stretch.

**P2. Two named people colliding.** "Meets", "vs", "first time", "return".
- Coded `first_meeting`: **31.5% (2.4×)**, n=20, 8 channels; 95% interval 16–58%, so strong but thin. IRL only: 33.8% (n=13).
- Title-wording version over everything: 19.7% (1.5×, n=193); IRL 25.5% (1.9×, n=106, just under the gate).
- Titles naming two streamers: 16.7% vs 8.7% for titles naming nobody.
- Why: the title asks a question only the video answers ("what happens when these two meet?").

### Leans (clear direction, not enough to call a rule)
**P3. Show a recognisable face in a real place, not a game frame.**
- No face detected: 6.5% (0.49×, n=431). Game-frame backgrounds: 5.4% (95% interval 3–8%) vs real-world backgrounds 19.2% (14–27%).
- The viewer is buying a person. A faceless or in-game thumbnail doesn't say who.
- Caveat: "game background" overlaps with the gaming and RP niches, which are weaker anyway.

**P4. The face sits in a scene; it doesn't fill the frame.** Biggest face 3–8% of the frame: 18.2% (1.37×); ≥8%: 12.6% (0.94×). Three or more faces: 17.5% (1.32×); one face: 12.3%. Groups and context beat a giant close-up.

**P5. Little or no thumbnail text.** No overlay text 17.1% (1.28×); 7+ words 11.8% (coded: 4.9%, n=24). Text that adds to the title did not help (9.6%, 0.72×).

**P6. Keep the title short and plain.** ≤40 characters 15.6% (1.17×); ≥80 characters 8.5% (0.64×); anything with a pipe or hashtag 9.7% (0.73×). No branding, series tags or keyword stuffing.

**P7. Many moments beat one event.** Supercuts (many clips of one streamer) 20.0% (interval 16–25%); single events 9.2% (7–12%); continuous single scenes overall 11.0%. The audience wants the best parts, not the whole encounter.

**P8. Footage type decides the base rate before any title trick.** IRL 18.1% (1.36×), gaming 11.5% (0.87×), **GTA RP 7.8% (0.59×)**.

### Not supported (these came from the earlier 6–25 min study)
- **"A face against one opposing thing":** 9.4% with an opposing element vs 13.0% without.
- **Arrows/circles** 8.7%, **cut-out designed thumbnails** 9.3% vs **raw unedited frames** 12.2%: extra design didn't pay.
- **Length promise ("for 10 minutes")**: 15.5% (1.17×); in gaming 10.9%, in RP too few. Fine, but not a driver.
- **Length itself:** no band beats baseline overall (6–9 min 14.0%, 9–12 13.5%, 12–30 13.8%). **3–6 min is the dead zone (8.2%).** The IRL 9–12 min band is stronger (20.2%, 1.5×, below the gate).
- **Questions in the title** 7.4% (n=54), **emoji** 11.6%, **ALL-CAPS words** 11.8%: no lift.

### Two kinds of big hit
1. **A repeatable format** run by a channel that does it every time. Outlier rate per channel: Clipped Vidz 37%, NeverMissClip 36%, SpeedEditsHD 32%, SilverClips 31%, shiifunnyasl 30%, ClipNeverStop 30%. Channels without a formula sit near 0–5% (Scotty, StreamerTV, Variety Roleplay, NoPixel Itch, GTA RP Clips, Jaron).
2. **A one-off moment a stranger can picture from the title** ("IShowSpeed SCARED To DEATH By BLACK ANIMATRONIC", 178×, 2 min; "CaseOh Reacts to Edits of Himself", 87×; "CaseOh Leaks His High School Pictures", 35×). These can't be scheduled; they come from what happens on stream.

**What the close-ups show (observations, 15 videos, not statistics):**
- **Thumbnails are cheap and literal.** Most are a raw or lightly captioned frame (facecams, a stream screenshot, a ring shot); none is a polished composite. Where there's a reaction, the thumbnail shows the person *and the thing they're reacting to* (fan art, the other streamer, the fight).
- **Videos start in the middle.** In the 7 videos with captions, none opens with an intro or greeting. They start mid-sentence or mid-bit ("I haven't done fan art in so long, bro"; "I got to go. Bye."; the ring announcer). Fanum states the premise and a stake in the first 5 s: "Fousey, funniest moments compilation number one. If I laugh, I'm a big baby."
- **One or two peaks, not an even curve.** "Most replayed" exists for 6 videos: peaks at 3:39 of a 10:07 fight, 8:58 of 10:12 (Fanum), 1:41 and 5:32 of 8 min (CaseOh), 0:00 and 5:41 (Speed, 8:29), 8:17 (Vyper). The title's promise is paid in one or two moments, often not in the first 30 s.
- Likes per view 0.8–2.3% (4.9% on the 1.9-min Stable Ronaldo short); comments 0.1–0.9 per 1,000 views, except the fight (4.8).
- Chapters exist on 7 of 15, mostly auto-generated.

## 3. What this means for us (options for the build plan, not decisions)

**Built (2026-11-24):** Option A is the `compilation` long kind (`clipper long check|render`, see `/clip-long`). It covers all streamer footage (IRL, just chatting, gaming, RP); the niche only adds notes. Every long kind gets Codex `$imagegen` thumbnails checked against section 1's thumbnail principles (`THUMB_PRINCIPLES` in `clipper/longform.py`), and the clip bank (`clipper bank`) keeps tagged moments after their sources are deleted.

Our one long video ("xQc Vs The Cops For 10 Minutes Straight 💀": 1,228 views, 17% average viewed) sits in the weakest cell: GTA RP footage (7.8% outlier rate) with a length-promise title (≤1.17×).

- **Option A, best supported:** on the `streamers` profile, from uploads and VODs, make "[named streamer] Funniest Moments (Compilation)" for IRL / just-chatting. Title ≤40 characters, one or two names. Thumbnail: a raw frame, the face(s) at 3–8% of the frame, real place, zero to three words. Open mid-moment; no intro. 8–12 min. Evidence: P1, P3–P7.
- **Option B, thinner evidence, cheap test:** a collision video ("X Meets Y For The 1st Time") from footage we already capture. Evidence: P2 (n=20 coded, 95% interval 16–58%); RP-only evidence is just n=4.
- **Option C, not recommended as the main bet:** more xQc NoPixel "for N minutes" running bits. The data gives it no lift.
- Either way, the first test should be small (3–4 videos per option) and judged on our own click-through and average viewed, which this study can't measure for other channels.

## 4. Limits (read before trusting a number)
- **Thumbnail coding covers 288 of 920 videos** (132 outliers + 156 normals), a random subset by contact-sheet. The coding agents were stopped by you and then hit the usage limit, so the other 632 are not coded. Face and text numbers use Apple Vision on all 3,461 and are not affected; the "opposing thing", expression, cut-out and setting numbers come from the 288 only.
- **Subjective thumbnail fields are noisy.** In a check of 12 coded rows against the images, objective fields (word count, people, background) were right on 11; expression and setting were debatable on 4. Expression cells (e.g. "anger" 60%, n=11) passed the gate on paper but are leads only, so I left anger out of the principles.
- **Title coding:** 20 random rows checked, no clear errors (1 debatable).
- **Cells overlap and are confounded** (niche, game backgrounds and compilation format move together); none of this is causal.
- **Outliers are measured within each channel;** no click-through or retention is available for other people's videos. "Most replayed" is only shown for 6 of 15 close-ups; captions were rate-limited by YouTube for 8, so first-30 s text exists for 7.
- **Channels were chosen by search and my judgement,** 46 in all; "IRL / gaming / RP" is a label per channel, not per video.
- The earlier study used a 5× cutoff and RP-heavy channels; this one uses 3× and a wider mix, which is why some of its thumbnail rules did not hold.
