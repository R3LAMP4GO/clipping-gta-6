# Long-form (6–25 min) streamer compilations: what outperforms (2026-11-18)

**Method.** 15 YouTube API searches for long streamer and GTA RP compilations posted in 2026 surfaced 27 third-party clip channels (Variety Roleplay, Buddha Clips, RP Domain, Moonbruh, Clipped Vidz, SpeedEditsHD, ImStillxQc, CaseOhs Orbit...). Each channel's last 150 uploads were pulled; every long video (6–25 min, 1,050 in total) was compared with the median of the 30 long videos the same channel posted around it, so channel size cancels out. **87 videos beat their channel's normal by 5× or more.** 15 of them, covering every format, were taken apart: thumbnail, chapters, YouTube's "most replayed" heatmap and the auto-captions of the first minute. Data: `long-form-outliers.json`.

## 2. What viewers wanted
- **The best of hours in minutes.** A 6-hour stream boiled down to its 25–35 strongest beats. The viewer gets the payoff without the dead air.
- **A guaranteed feeling.** The titles promise an emotion, not a topic ("Cure Your Depression", "You Can't Stop Laughing", "Perfect Timing"). People click for the mood.
- **Two personalities colliding.** First meetings and pairings (Cinna × Jynxzi, Speed × Jeffy, CaseOh + Jynxzi + Sketch): "what happens when these two meet?" is a question only the video answers.
- **Being in on the lore.** The RP outliers serve fans who follow characters and drama (CG metagaming, a newcomer's first 10 minutes on the server), like a recap of a show they watch.

## 3. Why it worked
- **One promise, kept again and again.** "Perfect Timing" and "HATING ON Spiderman" are one running bit, delivered 25+ times. Every clip pays off the title (a reward that comes back, in a slightly different form), so the viewer always knows what's coming next and wants another.
- **Hook = a flash-forward or the thesis line.** Jeffy opens with the best lines from later ("WHY ARE YOU TOUCHING ME") and then restarts from the beginning. Spider-Man opens on the rant itself. The RP encounters cold-open mid-scene. None open with an intro, a greeting or context.
- **Front-loaded, then a final spike.** In "Perfect Timing" the "most replayed" curve starts at 0.59 and falls to 0.07 by the end: the best clips go first. In the Spider-Man video the biggest spike is at 7:33, near the end, as a saved-for-last payoff. The ones that held best (Don't Scream Together: 0.43 halfway through) had a built-in conflict that keeps producing moments (in that game, making a noise gets you killed).
- **Two edit shapes win:**
  - **Supercut:** about 30 clips of 15–20 s in 8–10 min (27–37 gaps between clips).
  - **Single scene:** one continuous encounter of 6–12 min (3–15 gaps), for first meetings and confrontations.
  - Both stay short: the outlier median is 10 min vs 12 for the same channels' normal uploads.
- **Thumbnail = a face against one thing.** A huge cut-out face with an extreme expression next to one opposing element (an animatronic, a knife and doll, the Jeffy puppet, Spider-Man), 0–3 words. RP thumbnails put two streamer faces around the scene with a quote bubble ("IS THAT JYNXZI?!", "MY SHIT IS BIGGER THAN YOURS!").
- **Fewer comments, more likes.** Compilations get liked, not argued about: 0.56 comments per 1k views vs 1.29.

## 4. Our concept
Three formats, all built from what we already capture (xQc's NoPixel streams, plus Kai when he plays):
1. **Running-bit supercut:** "xQc's Jean Paul Lies To The Cops For 10 Minutes Straight 💀". One trait of the character, 25–30 clips, ordered to escalate. The same promise as "CaseOh HATING ON Spiderman for almost 9 minutes".
2. **First meeting / collision:** "[Streamer] Meets xQc For The 1st Time In NoPixel". One continuous encounter, 6–12 min. This is the strongest GTA RP signal (23% of RP outliers vs 3%).
3. **Feeling compilation:** "NoPixel Moments That Get Worse & Worse". The best 30 moments of the week from every stream we record, ordered by escalation.

## 5. Execution (format 1)
- **Thumbnail:** xQc cut out, mouth open in disbelief. Next to him one cop holding handcuffs, and a 2–3 word bubble ("I'M INNOCENT"). No logo, no more text.
- **Title:** "xQc Lies To The Cops For 10 Minutes Straight 💀 | NoPixel GTA RP".
- **0:00–0:15, flash-forward:** the three hardest lines from later clips, back to back, so the payoff is promised before anything is explained.
- **0:15–0:30, thesis:** xQc's own line that sets up the bit ("I've never seen this gun in my life"). No intro, no "what's up guys".
- **Body:** 25–30 clips of 15–30 s, ordered so each lie is bolder than the last. A small "LIE #7" counter is the recurring visual. Keep the facecam large. A bigger moment lands about every 2–3 min, matching the heatmap peaks.
- **Ending:** the best clip last, then end within 10 s. No outro: viewers leave once the bit ends (heatmaps fall to 0.0–0.1 at 90%).
- **Length:** 8–10 min.
- **Shorts link:** each Short from the same stream points to the long version ("full 10 min in the related video").

## 6. Test and iterate
- **Plan:** post format 1, 2 and 3 once each over two weeks, same streamer, at the same time of day.
- **Compare from our own YouTube Analytics at 48 h and 7 days:**
  - thumbnail click-through rate
  - share still watching at 30 s
  - average % viewed
  - where the retention curve dips (the clip there gets cut or moved next time)
- **Thumbnails:** run three per video with YouTube's thumbnail A/B test (Test & Compare in Studio).
- **Starting targets, to be revised from our own data:** about 5% click-through, 70% still watching at 30 s, 40% average viewed.
- **Next round:** keep the format and thumbnail style that win, and rebuild the losers around the winner's hook.
