description: Find the best GTA 6 moments in a YouTube video and render captioned vertical shorts with hook and title variants
argument-hint: [youtube-url | search phrase]
---

Find, review and render GTA 6 short-form clips with the local `clipper` CLI (run from the clipping-gta-6 project root). Everything runs locally (FFmpeg, yt-dlp, whisper.cpp, Demucs, WhisperKit). Read `research/viral-gta-shorts.md` before step 4; every hook, title, font and voiceover choice must follow it.

Input: $ARGUMENTS (a YouTube URL/id, a search phrase, or empty to scan the creators list).

1. **Setup questions.** Run `python3 -m clipper prefs show` for the saved defaults, then ask these in one go, with the saved value pre-selected or recommended. Ask with multiple-choice questions: in Claude Code use the AskUserQuestion tool; in Codex use request_user_input if available (Plan mode), otherwise list numbered options and wait for the reply.
   - **AI voiceover?** "No voiceover" / "Every video" / "Let the agent decide per video" (recommended: per the research, reactions skip it and context-less clips get one).
   - **How many finished videos?** "As many as are worth taking" (recommended: the analysis scores each source and recommends a count, so a 50-minute stream yields more than a 20-minute one) / 1 / 3 / 5.
   - **Hook variations per video?** "Let the data decide" (recommended: `auto` uses the count with the best breakout chance in `clipper simulate --strategies`, currently 3) / 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 (one MP4 per hook).
   - **Save these as defaults for scheduled runs?** Yes / No.
   If they choose to save, run `python3 -m clipper prefs set voice=<off|all|auto> videos_per_run=<auto|n> hooks_per_video=<auto|n>`.
2. **Find a video on the profile's topic.** With no URL, run `python3 -m clipper watch` (add `--search "<phrase>"` for a phrase). In `gta6` it keeps only videos whose title, description or tags prove they are about GTA 6; `--profile streamers` keeps everything else the same streamers post (other games, IRL, just chatting) and drops GTA 6 titles. Show a table (channel, title, length, id, evidence, processed) and ask which one to clip.
3. **Fetch + analyze.** `python3 -m clipper run <url-or-id>` (for streams over ~2h add `--section H:MM:SS-H:MM:SS`). This writes `jobs/<id>/candidates.md` plus contact sheets.
4. **Review like a viral editor.** Read `candidates.md`: its header says how many videos are worth exporting and whether the source is a reaction or info video. Each candidate has a tier (strong / maybe / skip) based on the signals the 500k+ Shorts shared: sudden volume jumps (screams, reactions), loudness peaks, hype words, sound events and most-replayed data. View each `review/cand_NN.jpg` and read the word timings in `transcript.json` around the best moments (one word per line, `{"w", "s", "e"}` in source seconds: Grep for `"s": 843.` to find second 843, then Read the lines around it). Re-rank by the research: start ON the reaction or the setup line (cold open); the moment must pay off a question a viewer would have; works without context. For every moment, name its **click trigger** from the research "Outlier study": a curiosity gap, the viewer as judge, play-along/streak, suspense (we know more than the target), irony against the persona, a shock confession, status/celebrity, or an absurd one-line premise. No nameable trigger, no clip. **Own channel winners (2026-11-23).** What separated our 4k–28k-view Shorts from the ones stuck at ~1k (the top 10 all paid off by ~16 s and ended on it, 89–137% average viewed; the 1k-stuck ones kept 0.43 of viewers at 90% of the clip vs 0.69 for breakouts). Measured on GTA 6 clips; the same rules apply to every profile (other games, IRL, just chatting) until `clipper stats` per profile confirms or corrects them. (a) **One cold-readable beat:** real-world money or a price ("ONE GUN COSTS *$590*?!", Speed paying $2,000 for a jacket), a first (Kai's first GTA 6 mission, Speed's first time in Tokyo), a chase/escape or confrontation (cops chasing xQc, security throwing Kai out of a store), a theft (stealing a car off a moving truck, a shoplifter on stream), or a blunt verdict ("this is the worst game I've ever played"). (b) **Payoff by ~16 s at the latest**, ending within ~5 s after it so the clip loops (`clipper check` notes a payoff past 20 s and a tail past 5 s). (c) **12–25 s long, with the streamer talking or reacting throughout**: set `"streamer_reacts": false` honestly when they go quiet for long stretches (`clipper check` notes it). (d) **Avoid:** cutscenes or scenes where only other people talk, quizzes, sentimental moments, inside jokes or ongoing storylines, and titles the clip never answers. **Then the 100K+ moment rules (research `100k-profile-2026-11.md`, 2026-11-25).** These come from 648 clips with 100K+ views (5× their channel) against 1,312 flops from the same 25 clip channels, with 299 of them measured frame by frame, so channel size cancels out. **(1) Show the cause, not just the reaction:** 100K+ clips show what happened *and* the reaction 89% of the time vs 81% for flops (1.54×, the clearest moment trait), and reaction-only clips lean worse (0.63×). Each candidate in `candidates.md` has a **Lead-up** block: the 45 s before its loudest peak, with `[reaction]` marking the lines that are only reactions. Find the cause in the unmarked lines and start early enough to show it. **Select the cause of the reaction, not the reaction itself** (user's Principle 1): weak → strong: "Kai starts screaming" → "Kai discovers his Minecraft world was deleted"; "xQc starts laughing" → "an NPC charges xQc an absurd amount for an Uber"; "Speed looks surprised" → "Speed accidentally wakes up the wrong person"; "Kai gets angry" → "Fanum confronts Kai and demands an apology"; "a streamer reacts to GTA" → "a streamer discovers an unexpected GTA feature". Describe the event without crazy, hilarious, insane, unbelievable or funny; if the description is no longer interesting without those words, downgrade the clip. **(2) Pick a moment from the seven categories, in this priority order, and name its `category`** (user's Principle 2, 2026-11-25). A moment that fits none of them isn't clipped. (a) `misunderstanding`: someone believes something that turns out to be wrong. Look for mistaken identity, wrong assumptions, accidental revelations and dramatic irony ("Rakai Accidentally Woke Up Rayquan" 95.6M, "Speed & Kai Got Fooled by Jamal's Prank" 34.0M). (b) `chemistry`: an interaction shows an entertaining relationship between two or more personalities. Look for roasting, teasing, disagreements, unexpected honesty, friendship, rivalry and role reversals ("Max Did Not Expect Ray to Become a Diva" 90.1M, "Fanum Presses Lil RT & Kai Cenat" 70.2M). (c) `consequence`: something happens at an unusually large scale or with a real consequence. Look for complete losses, improbable wins, hard challenges, large sums of money and consequential decisions ("Jamal Deleted Speed and Kai's Entire Minecraft World" 15.0M, "Kai Cenat Beat All GTA Games" 1.7M). (d) `character_behaviour`: a distinctive behaviour that's entertaining because of who does it. Look for strange habits, recurring jokes, exaggerated personality traits and recognisable patterns ("Rakai Always Wearing Double Durags" 68.9M, "IShowSpeed Always Has His Headset On" 24.7M). (e) `discovery`: a streamer discovers something the audience would also find interesting. Look for hidden mechanics, unusual NPC behaviour, surprising information, Easter eggs and unknown features ("Kai Finds Out La Peace Stops Babies Crying" 14.4M, "Kai Cenat Leaked GTA 6 Info, Part 2" 1.8M). (f) `crossover`: a streamer meets a recognisable personality, brand, game, meme or cultural event. Look for celebrities, trending memes, popular songs, major games and unexpected collaborations ("Kai Cenat Met Ice Spice in GTA" 20.6M, "Speed Reacts to Portugal's Song" 18.3M). (g) `reward`: a moment delivers happiness, admiration, justice, generosity or a satisfying conclusion. Look for meaningful surprises, wholesome moments, reunions, standing up for someone and satisfying resolutions ("Kai Cenat Giving a Great Lesson" 72.2M, "Ron Surprises Lacy With a PS5 Pro" 3.6M). When two moments are otherwise equal, take the one higher on this list. `clipper check` fails any other `category`. **(2b) Find the meaningful contrast** (user's Principle 3). An interesting moment usually holds a contrast between two things; name its `contrast` type: `expectation_reality` (Rakai wakes up the wrong person), `normal_absurd` (an unusually expensive in-game Uber), `intention_outcome` (someone attempts a prank that backfires), `personality_behaviour` (a competitive streamer acts completely indifferent), `knowledge_ignorance` (the audience knows something the streamer doesn't), `status_situation` (a powerful character is humiliated by an NPC) or `desire_obstacle` (Kai needs $2 million but can't sell his supercar). Say exactly what two things contrast and why that would entertain GTA viewers. Contrast isn't mandatory: genuine friendship, impressive accomplishments and other rewarding moments can work without it (leave `contrast` out; `clipper check` only notes it). **(2d) Prioritise audience-specific novelty** (user's Principle 4): ask what this clip offers that GTA viewers haven't already seen hundreds of times, judged against what they already know, not the general public. Lower → higher priority: drives a car → discovers an unusual driving mechanic; fights police → escapes police in an unexpected way; talks to an NPC → the NPC responds in an unusually realistic way; buys something → discovers a ridiculous price; reacts to gameplay → discovers something players haven't seen; loses a fight → loses because of an entertaining misunderstanding. Write the answer in `novelty` (`clipper check` fails a pick without one). **(2c) Select moments that develop** (user's Principle 5). A clip is stronger when something changes during it: **initial situation** (Kai books an unusually expensive Uber) → **complication** (who could possibly be driving it?) → **revelation** (the driver or situation is revealed) → **payoff** (Kai's entertaining reaction). Start the clip on the initial situation and end a few seconds after the payoff. Write the four beats in `arc`. Reject conversations that stay in essentially the same state throughout, unless the dialogue itself is exceptionally entertaining. **(3) Write the `cause`:** the event in plain words, at most 16, with no hype adjectives and not the reaction ("a fan hands Kai a $5,000 watch", not "Kai's craziest reaction"). Also write `why_care`, one line on why a stranger scrolling past would care. `clipper check` fails a missing or hype-filled cause, or one that only names a reaction. **(4) Score the `review`** honestly, 0–10 each, in two stages (user's evaluation system; the weights are proposed starting values in `review_weights`). **Stage A, moment quality:** `demand` (20: the subject has shown interest with this audience; use the **Demand** line in `candidates.md`), `entertainment` (25: the event stays interesting without editing or exaggerated language), `specificity` (10: a concrete action, discovery, consequence or interaction), `distinctiveness` (15: more than routine streamer behaviour), `development` (15: the situation changes, escalates or reveals something) and `payoff` (15: an entertaining or satisfying outcome). **Stage B, opening and packaging:** `interest` (25: the opening introduces a genuinely interesting event), `text_hook` (20: the text names a specific fact, contrast or stakes), `context` (15: the audience understands why it matters), `visual` (10: the opening visuals establish the event), `anticipation` (15: a reason to keep watching) and `alignment` (15: the footage delivers what the hook promises). `clipper check` weights them (`review_weights` in `watchlist.yaml`) and decides from the **moment score first**, so packaging can't carry a weak moment. 70+ passes. 60–69 is the `test` tier, which renders but posts only while the account is under `min_posts_per_day`. Under 60, reject. With `principles: shadow` a low score is a note; with `on` it fails. **(5) The 100K+ profile check** (`profile` in `clipper check`) compares each pick with the clear and lean traits: the split facecam layout when the stream has a cam (1.42×), at least one 2–6 word situation-label hook (1.31×), and opening text present (no text 0.72×). Three measured traits are **notes only**: talking speed (100K+ clips talk a little slower, at most ~3.1 words a second, 0.87×), the loudest jump landing later rather than in the first seconds (1.18×) and length (1.09×; our own 12–25 s winners contradict an 18 s floor). As hard cutoffs, talking speed and jump timing rejected 38% of the measured 100K+ clips against 48% of flops, so treat them as tie-breakers, not gates. It reports `match` as clear traits met out of those measured. Payoff time, tail, ending type, captions and flashes showed **no difference** between 100K+ clips and flops, so they aren't profile rules. The payoff-by-~16 s and end-within-5 s notes above come from our own channel and stay as notes. Old picks with `premise_type` / `selection` and no `review` still check under the old gate. Write new picks with `category`, `cause`, `why_care` and `review`. The 100K+ examples per category and streamer are in `research/100k-examples.json`: read the ones for this moment's category and streamer before writing hooks. Pick the moment for its payoff first, then decide where it starts. Then two gates every pick must pass (research "Hits vs flops: the idea", 2026-11-19): **(1) a turn**: something changes between people (a gift, prank, betrayal, meeting someone, a confrontation, a dare) or a visible act with stakes happens (a theft, a chase, money spent); **(2) a cold premise**: write the premise in 12 words or fewer for someone who has never seen the stream. If it needs a roleplay character name, a crew, a business plot or earlier events, skip it, and keep the hook and titles just as cold (no roleplay names, no "xQc's crew"; `clipper check` notes them): our lore clips ("OG ordered", Mickey's crew, Lee's USB) got 100-600 views while cold-readable events (xQc steals a Corvette with cops searching, Kai finds out a player is 13) got 7-15k. Skip by default: trailer reactions (the flop on two separate 100K+ channels), rambling opinions and comment-reading clips (a blunt verdict under 20 s is fine), and roleplay POV memes. Prefer Kai Cenat, IShowSpeed and CaseOh over everyone else, and GTA 6 over NoPixel. On the 25 clip channels, CaseOh clips were measurably more often 100K+ (1.32×) and Lacy clips less often (0.73×); Kai and Speed were at their channels' norm. The Demand line in `candidates.md` shows each named streamer's rate. The tier is a weak guide: on 83 outlier-vs-flop pairs, loudness picked the hit 63-69% of the time and hype-word density picked the flop more often than the hit (`clipper bench --moments`), so don't count chatter ("bro", "yo", "wait") as evidence. The premise must land in the first second, and the clip should end on the face. Hook and title state the situation and withhold the outcome. Also read the **Scenario candidates** section and hunt for **curiosity gaps** anywhere in the transcript: the streamer makes, finds, tries or shows something new (a combo, trick, secret spot, custom car or room, build, first try, a real-life thing recreated in the game) and the outcome stays unknown until a reveal or a face. They often have no volume spike, so judge them from the transcript and contact sheet (research "Scenario case study"). Their hooks name the new thing and withhold what it is ("What new *combo* did Kai just invent?"). **Length is your call per moment**, from the analysis (the `candidates.md` aim, the clip_type's playbook length) and where the payoff actually lands: any length up to **45s maximum**, as short as the moment allows: aim for 12–25 s (our 4k–28k-view Shorts ran 12–25 s; 500K+ clip Shorts: median 23 s; our 40 s+ uploads got about 1.1k views). Go past 25 s only while a streak or story keeps escalating; `clipper check` notes it. Only take a moment that is worth posting. With "as many as worth taking", export the recommended count, minus any you judge weak, plus a skip-tier moment only if you can say why it works. Present the picks as a table (rank, source timestamp + link, length, format, voiceover yes/no + why, why it works) and ask the user to approve, trim or swap.
   **Why outliers work (research/outlier-questions-2026-11.md).** A clip sells one question the viewer can't answer without watching. Pick the question first, then the moment that answers it, and record it as `question`:

   | `question` | The viewer's question | GTA 6 | Streamers / IRL |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | `choice` | Does it react if I do X? | "if you pick up Lucia in a sports car this happens" (304K, ~39x) | Speed asks a stranger for $1,000 in Tokyo |
   | `tries` | How badly will this go? | "Awkward NPC tries to rob Lucia" (78K, ~130x) | a fan tries to prank Kai on stream and gets caught |
   | `collision` | Who snaps first? | "What happens when Lucia finds out about Mandy" (77K, ~11x) | two streamers with beef meet live |
   | `recognition` | Is that real? | "Speed finds a Hulk-like monster" (4.3M, ~22x) | Speed meets Ronaldo on a random street |
   | `event` | What is this world? | "Lucia Joins A Cult" (279K, ~9x) | Speed wins Streamer of the Year, a marathon's 24-hour mark |

   Measured on 2,910 titles from 46 GTA 6 and streamer clip channels, only `event` beat a plain description by 2x. That was in streamer Shorts (36% vs 17% outliers), and it was mostly Streamer Awards clips, so the event has to be happening now. `recognition` came next (1.39x). The other types, and question-frame titles ("What happens if…", "X tries to…"), showed no reliable edge, so titles still narrate the setup as step 7 says. The surprise has to be real: a myth everyone expects (Bigfoot) got 14K next to Hulk's 4.3M. `clipper stats` compares the five on our channel.

5. **Voiceover key (only if any approved video has a voiceover).** Run `python3 -m clipper voice-setup` with a 10-minute timeout. It checks any saved key; otherwise it opens the Fish Audio API-key page (free developer tier) and a temporary TextEdit note. Tell the user: "Click Get your API key, paste it into the TextEdit note, and press Cmd+S." The command verifies the key, saves it to `.env`, and deletes the note. Never print the key. If they'd rather not, drop the voiceovers.
6. **Choose a layout.** If a facecam is visible anywhere in the stream (most streamer VODs, including a small cam in a corner like xQc's own uploads), run `python3 -m clipper frame <id> <seconds>`, read the gridded frame, and use `"layout": {"type": "split", "face": [x, y, w, h]}` (fractions). Use `{"type": "fit"}` only when there is no facecam. Faceless openings kept 38% of viewers vs 50% with a face, and half the views. `clipper check --job` fails a fit clip from a channel whose earlier clips used split; if the cam really is off or covered in that stretch, write `{"type": "fit", "facecam": "hidden"}`. In a profile with `require_facecam: true` (gta-rp) every clip must show the face: skip moments where the cam is off, because the check fails any fit clip there, hidden or not.
7. **Write picks.** Save `jobs/<id>/picks.json`, a list with one entry per video:
   `{"start", "end", "clip_type", "layout", "hook_style", "caption_style", "hooks": [N hooks], "titles": [4 titles], "description", "hashtags", "voiceover" (only if chosen), "narrate" (optional), "labels" (optional), "turn", "cold_premise", "premise_type", "question", "selection", "loop", "payoff_at", "streamer_reacts", "captions" (optional)}`.
   - **turn / cold_premise (required):** the two gates from step 4, written down: `"turn": "Ron hands Lacy a PS5 Pro"`, `"cold_premise": "A friend surprises a streamer with the console he wanted"` (12 words max, no lore). `clipper check` fails a pick missing either, and `clipper stats` later compares how clips with each kind of turn did.
   - **category / cause / why_care / review (required for new picks):** from step 4's 100K+ moment rules: `"category": "discovery"`, `"cause": "a fan hands Kai a $5,000 watch"`, `"why_care": "a stranger gives away a $5,000 watch on stream"`, and `"review": {"moment": {"demand": 6, "entertainment": 8, "specificity": 8, "distinctiveness": 7, "development": 7, "payoff": 8}, "packaging": {"interest": 8, "text_hook": 7, "context": 8, "visual": 7, "anticipation": 8, "alignment": 8}}`. Optionally add `premise_at` (source seconds when a stranger understands the situation) and `setup_start` (an earlier start on the setup line, used only by the `opening` experiment). They go into `upload.json`, and the autopilot orders posts by the moment score and profile match once `principles` is `on`.
   - **contrast / arc (required for new picks):** from step 4's Principles 3 and 5: `"contrast": {"type": "normal_absurd", "what": "a normal Uber ride costs $339"}` and `"arc": {"situation": "Kai books an Uber", "complication": "the price is $339", "revelation": "who the driver is", "payoff": "Kai's reaction"}`, all four beats different. `clipper check` fails a missing or unknown contrast type and an arc missing a beat; both go into `upload.json` (`contrast_type`, `arc`) so `clipper stats` compares contrast types on our channel.
   - **question:** which of `choice`, `tries`, `collision`, `recognition` or `event` the clip answers (step 4, "Why outliers work"). `clipper check` fails any other value and notes a missing one; it goes into `upload.json` so `clipper stats` can compare them.
   - **Reframe every pick (required; research "Open loops", 2026-11-19).** Any moment can be framed so a stranger wants to see how it ends, as long as the footage shows the ending. Four steps:
     1. **Find the most extreme, picturable fact in the clip**: the claymores on the door, the ransom that is two bags of chips, his photo on the store wall, his mom grounding him mid-robbery. Not the streamer's mood, not the game.
     2. **Open the loop on that fact and withhold the outcome.** Best frames, measured against each channel's own norm: a *told setup with the ending withheld* ("He Took A Hostage And Only Asked For Two Bags Of Chips 😭", "Kai Cenat Wanted To Prank IShowSpeed"; 1.13x on 27 streamer-clip channels) or a *hypothetical* the viewer can put themselves in ("What Happens If The Ransom Is Just Chips?", "What Happens If Arthur Drinks 99 Hair Tonics?" 9.9M; 1.12x on 30 story-game channels). A question works when the answer is something you see ("Why is xQc's *photo* on this wall?"). Avoid "did you know" (0.71x) and bare teases (0.86-0.97x). The frame only works when the fact is extreme (99, every, never, only, kill) and needs no lore; the flops used the same frames on small facts ("What happens if you donate in Saint Denis?", 0.16x).
     3. **Open on the line or image that states the fact** (`start` on "I want two bags of chips from that vending machine", or the photo on the wall), never on lore or jargon ("he got OG ordered"); add a `labels` entry when a word needs translating (`*HIS MOM*`).
     4. **End on the outcome**, a few seconds after it lands. If the stream shows it later, make it a segment pick with the outcome last. No outcome in the footage, no clip: an open loop that never closes is the clickbait research rule 8 bans.
     Write it down: `"loop": "will they really trade the hostage for chips?"` (the question a stranger has after the first second) and `"payoff_at": 1234.5` (when the clip answers it; source seconds, clip seconds for a segment pick, like labels). `clipper check` fails a pick missing either or with the payoff outside the clip, notes one that closes in the first half, and `clipper stats` compares the frames on our channel.
   - **streamer_reacts:** `true` when the streamer talks or reacts through the whole clip, `false` when a cutscene or other people carry it (`clipper check` notes `false`: those stuck at ~1k on our channel). Be honest; it's also how `clipper stats` learns whether the rule holds outside GTA 6.
   - **captions (optional):** `false` when the source already burns in subtitles (a trailer watched with subtitles on): two sets would stack over the game. Action labels still show.
   - **narrate (narrated explainer, optional):** for a moment that only lands with context (a lore story, a chain of events across a stream), write the narrator's script in `voiceover` and set `"narrate": true`. The Fish Audio voice talks over the first part of the clip with the game audio ducked, the captions follow the script word by word, and the streamer's own voice comes back for the payoff. Open with the promise itself, in the forms the narrated outliers use ("Did you know…", "Here are the 3…", "N years later…"; research "Story-game Shorts outliers"), tell the setup in 2–4 short sentences over matching segments, and stop before the punchline. About 2.6 words a second: `clipper check` fails narration longer than the clip and one that leaves under 3 s for the streamer's payoff. Reactions whose humour is the streamer's own voice stay un-narrated.
   - **Three kinds of video, all welcome** (YouTube's originality rules favour edited videos over raw re-uploads; research "Originality"):
     - **Single clip** (`start`/`end`): one moment, as above. Still the default when one moment carries itself.
     - **Story cut**: several moments of one stream in one Short (setup → turn → payoff, e.g. "Moment of truth" → the blackout → "y'all missed that entire thing?"). Use `"segments": [{"start", "end"}, ...]` (source seconds, in play order) instead of `start`/`end`. Cut dead air between beats; every segment must earn its seconds.
     - **Multi-streamer edit**: the same moment from several streams (same cutscene, same heist, same reveal). Run `python3 -m clipper find "<a line said in it>"` (searches every stream recorded or fetched in the last 6 h; `--hours N`): GTA cutscene dialogue is identical in every stream, so one line finds the same moment everywhere, with each job's source seconds. Add `"job": "<that job>"` (and its own `"layout"` if its facecam sits elsewhere: `clipper frame <that job> <s>`) to the segments from other streams. Every source is credited in the description.
     - The **45s maximum** counts all segments together. A segment pick's `labels` use seconds of the finished clip (0 = its first frame).
   - **Never reuse footage:** don't pick a moment (or segment) already used by an exported clip; the same footage gets no reach the second time. `clipper check --job <id>` fails any pick that shares more than 3 s with a clip this profile already exported.
   - **labels (context captions):** timed captions in asterisks that say what's happening (`*CONFUSED*`, `*FOLLOWS*`, `*LAST MISSION*`, `*in disbelief*`, `*GETS CLOSER*`): `[{"s": <source seconds>, "e": <source seconds>, "text": "*in disbelief*"}]`. About 1 in 4 of the 500K+ gaming clip Shorts use them, mostly over quiet stretches, so a viewer who doesn't know the streamer can follow with the sound off. Use one in the first 3 s when nobody talks or the situation isn't obvious (`clipper check` notes a clip without one), and at key silent beats toward the payoff. Use 1–4 words, present tense. A label shows over speech (the speech captions hide while it's up), so keep each to about 1–2 s and never cover the punchline.
   - **Payoff ending (research "Zylok teardown"):** cold-open on the first meaningful word (never "so", "um", "great, the…") and end on the **result**, not the decision or the take. If the stream shows the outcome later (a death, a crash, the reveal), make it a segment pick and add the outcome as the last segment with a result label (`*MICHAEL DEAD*`). A clip that only shows the choice ("Kai picks who to kill") underperforms. The hook must never say the outcome; the title names the event (the title principles below). `clipper check` notes a filler opening, a hook that repeats words from the ending, and 2s+ dead air (cut it with segments).
   - **caption_fixes:** read the clip's transcript lines and fix misheard names: `"caption_fixes": {"swerver": "Trevor"}` (whole words, any case; the profile's `caption_fixes` already covers common GTA names). Swears in captions are censored automatically (`F*CK`); the audio is untouched.
   - **fx (optional):** edit beats in source seconds (clip seconds for segment picks, like labels): `"fx": [{"t": 12.4, "type": "flash"}, {"t": 15.0, "type": "zoom"}, {"t": 21.0, "type": "hit"}]`. `flash` is a quick lime wash for the payoff, `zoom` a punch-in on a shouted line, `hit` a sound hit; a hit can name its sound with `"sound": "boom"` (or `hit`, `whoosh`; any approved `kind: hit` id in `assets/music/library.yaml`, otherwise a random one). Leave `fx` out and the render adds zooms on shouted lines ("!") and a white flash plus a hit at the start of a segment pick's last segment; `"fx": []` means no effects on purpose.
   - **music (optional):** `"music": [{"s", "e", "mood"}]` windows (same seconds as labels) for quiet stretches; mood is `suspense`, `sneaky`, `epic`, `sad` or `funny`. Music sits low under speech and comes up where nobody talks. Only approved tracks from `assets/music/library.yaml` play; with none approved for that mood, the window is silently skipped. `"music": []` means no added audio on purpose (no music, and no auto hit).
   - **Add effects only when they add** (from 25 Zylok Shorts, research "Zylok channel survey"). They usually do: most clips should get some. Decide per clip:
     - **Sound hits:** a `boom` on the payoff and on the one or two biggest reactions or punchlines, a `whoosh` on a hard cut between segments. At most about 1 per 4s; Zylok averages 1 per 19s and never more than 3 a clip. Not on every line.
     - **Visual fx:** a `flash` on the payoff or a hard cut, a `zoom` on a shouted line. Short and frequent, never long.
     - **Flash colour** (`"color"` on a flash: `white`, `red`, `orange`, `lime` or hex like `#FF0000`): white for a reveal or shock, red/orange for danger, a death or a crash, lime (the default for a flash you add without a color) as the brand colour.
     - **Music:** only on a speech-free stretch of 2s or more, or under a tense, funny or emotional build-up. Pick the mood from the moment (`sad` for a heartfelt beat, `funny` for a goofy gap, `suspense` before a reveal, `epic` for a big launch); meme music is optional, never the default. Skip it when the game's own music or score is already loud, or when talk fills the whole clip: Zylok puts music on only about 1 clip in 5. `clipper check` notes a 3s+ speech-free stretch with no `music` key; add a window or `"music": []`.
   - **clip_type first:** `reaction`, `comedy`, `news`, `gameplay`, `showcase` or `scenario`, decided per *moment* (`candidates.md` suggests one per source, and the research "Playbook" table defines them). It sets the hook structures in order, hook_style, caption_style, the voiceover default and the length. Example: xQc's hostages singing harmonies is `comedy` (meme box, relatable/tease hooks), not a reaction.
   - **Opening text sets up what's about to happen; it never announces the reaction (2026-11-21).** Our openings narrate how someone reacts ("xQc did NOT expect this from the PD", 636 views; "xQc did NOT leave quietly", 654); 100K+ GTA openings label the situation (`*EASIEST MISSION*`, `*TRAIN MISSION*`, `*PUTS ON NIKE*`, `*IN TRAFFIC*`) and let the footage show what happens. Name the concrete detail, contrast or stakes ("ONE GUN COSTS $590?!" not "xQc did NOT see this price coming"; "HE'S ONLY 13?!"; "DRIVING IN SCUBA GEAR"), and only words the footage backs up. **Banned openers** (`clipper check` fails them and the render drops them): "Nobody…", "NOT ready", "did NOT", "didn't expect", "could NOT believe", "was NOT…", "When…", "Me…", "POV:" and any other line that explains a reaction; write a 2–6 word setup label instead ("THE FINAL *BRIDGE* SHOOTOUT", "STEALING HIS OWN *UBER*"). No emoji in hooks: the on-screen font has none and burns them in as empty boxes (titles keep theirs). `clipper check` notes a title that announces a reaction with no concrete detail. **Hook-angle test:** every clip with 3+ hooks needs one of each angle: a **label** (2–6 word situation that sets up the contrast: "THE EASIEST MISSION") and a **detail** (the concrete surprise: "ONE GUN COSTS $590?!"). No reaction hooks (the reaction arm was dropped 2026-11-21). The render picks which angle leads each video from its footage (about half each) and records it in `upload.json` (`angle_arm`, `hook_angle`), so `clipper stats` compares the angles on views, lift, viewed-vs-swiped and retention. Shorter text is not proven to win on its own: our hooks above and below 3K views both had a median of 10 words.
   - **hooks:** the chosen number, each a **different** structure, best first by measured effect on 3,481 gaming Shorts (`python3 -m clipper simulate` prints the table): number promise 2.5x, shock fact 1.7x, relatable situation 1.5x, withheld/negation 1.4x, question 1.2x, tease 1.2x (only when it says what to watch), superlative 1.1x. Naming the streamer adds 1.2x ("Nobody expected xQc's hostages to *SING*"). Never use: vague hooks with one content word ("Wait for the harmony...", 0.67x), bare topic labels (0.88x), announcement wording (0.85x). First-person claims (0.94x) and a CAPS word with no other structure (0.97x) don't help. Never spoil the clip ("… Reaction"). A plain line that tells the setup and holds back the ending is fine ("Ron tried to surprise Lacy with a *PS5 Pro*"); `clipper check` notes one with no open loop: 100K+ clip channels rarely use questions (2%) or negations (5%), and our titles over-used them (25% and 46%). 2–6 words (relatable up to 9), one keyword marked `*like this*`. Each hook becomes a file name, so keep them distinct.
   - **Text hook principles (user, 2026-11-25).** The text hook does a different job from the title: it makes someone who's already seeing the video understand why to keep watching. (1) **Reveal the most interesting detail immediately.** Our better hooks name a specific unusual fact, problem or outcome ("Who's driving Kai's $339 Uber?" 18,966 first-day views; "xQc paid $24 for a GTA 6 Uber" 13,742). Artificial → substantive: "Wait until the end" → "Kai just paid $339 for an Uber"; "You won't believe this" → "He thinks he's talking to Ice Spice"; "Watch what happens next" → "Kai needs $2 million before the mission"; "This NPC is crazy" → "This NPC refuses to let Kai leave"; "Kai wasn't ready for this" → "Kai didn't know there was a second $100K vault". `clipper check` fails artificial curiosity with nothing concrete. (2) **Create a specific mental question**, even without a question mark ("Kai just paid $339 for an Uber" → why is it that expensive?; "Rakai woke up the wrong person" → how will that person react?; "Fanum wants Kai to apologize" → what did Kai do?). Write it in `hook_questions` before approving the hook; if there's no reason to keep watching, the hook is weak. (3) **Complement the opening footage.** The visual shows the situation, the spoken line advances it, and the text supplies what the footage can't ("He paid $339 for this Uber", not "xQc starts laughing 💀"). `clipper check` fails a hook that only repeats the visible reaction. (4) **Generate different psychological angles, not different wording.** For Kai's $339 Uber: `shock_fact` "Kai just paid $339 for an Uber", `mystery` "Who's driving Kai's $339 Uber?", `relatability` "GTA Uber prices are getting out of hand", `suspicion` "Kai did NOT trust this Uber driver", `anticipation` "Watch who pulls up in Kai's $339 Uber", `contrast` "A $339 Uber in a game about stealing cars". Use only angles the footage backs (no `suspicion` unless the streamer really shows distrust) and lead with the one that best matches the footage and payoff.
   - **hook_angles / hook_questions (required for new picks):** one entry per hook, same order. `hook_angles` uses the psychological angles above (`shock_fact`, `mystery`, `relatability`, `suspicion`, `anticipation`, `contrast`), or `label` (a 2–6 word situation label, the measured 100K+ winner), `detail`, `question` or `stakes`; with 3+ hooks each needs a different angle, and keep one label and one detail-style hook for the hook-angle test. `hook_questions` gives the question each hook puts in a stranger's head, all different. Generic hooks with no concrete word ("WAIT FOR IT", "THIS IS CRAZY") fail. 100K+ openings almost always carry text (96% vs 88%), most often a short situation label.
   - **hook_style:** `big` for every clip type (the 1M+ clip channels' look: a big bold premise, at most 2 lines; in split it stands on the seam over the bottom of the facecam and captions sit on the game's top edge, so text never covers the gameplay). Half the clips render with no banner at all (`no_hook_share: 0.5`), so big hook vs none gets an A/B answer. `bold`, `box` and `label` still render if a pick asks for them.
   - **caption_style:** `boxed` (Bebas on black) for streamer reactions, `bold` for commentary/news, `comic` for playful gameplay.
   - **Choosing hooks:** draft one hook for every type that honestly fits the clip_type (research "Title formulas" table; never invent a number or a list the clip doesn't have), run `python3 -m clipper simulate jobs/<id>/picks.json` (a tie-breaker between hooks you already like: on clip-channel data it told hits from flops at chance, AUC 0.53), and keep the hooks on its `keep` line (it uses `hooks_per_video`, or the data-chosen count when that is `auto`), each a different type, best first. Don't give every clip every type: forcing misfits lost in every simulated scenario. But draft every type that fits, at least 3, before simulating (the playbook row for its clip_type lists 4 in order; a reaction now leads with a superlative or shock-fact setup ("THE *EASIEST* MISSION IN GTA 6", "One gun costs *$590* in GTA 6"), then a question; never a reaction opener), so `keep` can fill `hooks_per_video`. Fewer than 3 only when you can name why the others don't fit, and say so in the picks table.
   - **titles:** one per hook, same order. **Title principles (user, 2026-11-25), weak → improved:** (1) **name the actual event**: "Kai Goes Crazy" → "Kai Finds Out His Minecraft World Was Deleted"; (2) **communicate concrete stakes**: "xQc Is In Trouble" → "xQc Has 30 Seconds to Escape the Cops"; (3) **highlight the unusual detail**: "Rakai Is So Funny" → "Rakai Always Wears Two Durags"; (4) **emphasise character dynamics**: "Kai and Fanum Funny Moment" → "Fanum Forces Kai to Apologize"; (5) **include recognisable subjects**: "Ron Surprises Lacy" → "Ron Surprises Lacy With a PS5 Pro"; (6) **quantify meaningful extremes**: "Kai Needs Money" → "Kai Needs $2 Million but Can't Sell His Car"; (7) **remove empty exaggeration**: "This GTA Moment Was INSANE" → "GTA 6 Charges Kai $339 for an Uber". `clipper check` fails empty exaggeration (insane, crazy, wild, so funny, funny moment, goes crazy, in trouble…) and notes a title too vague to say what happened (no number or amount, two content words or fewer, and not two named people). **Five title formulas:** unexpected event ([Streamer] + [unexpected action or event]), consequence ([Person] + [action] + [meaningful consequence]), discovery ([Streamer] finds out [surprising information]), character dynamic ([Person A] + [interesting interaction] + [Person B]) and extraordinary scale ([Streamer] + [unusual quantity or accomplishment]). **The rule:** reveal enough to make the situation interesting and leave the experience of watching it unfold to the video: saying someone deleted a Minecraft world doesn't spoil it, viewers still want the reaction. About 87% of both 100K+ clips and flops use direct, descriptive titles, so the structure isn't what separates them; what the title communicates is. Use specific details only when the footage backs them. State the specific event, not a reaction to it ("Kai Cenat Fails The Easiest Mission In GTA 5", not "Kai Cenat Was NOT Ready For This Mission"): the streamer first, the event on the clip's most extreme, picturable fact, one emoji; past tense measured slightly better on 100K+ clips (1.13×), present tense is fine. "Kai Cenat Tried To Steal A Car Off A Moving Truck In GTA 6 😱", "xQc Refused To Give An Armed Robber $5 In GTA 6 😭", "He Took A Hostage And Only Asked For Two Bags Of Chips 😭"; or a hypothetical the viewer can put themselves in ("What Happens If You Rob The First Guy You See In GTA 6?"). Don't use "Me…", "POV:", "When you…" or "Us…" for streamer clips: they hide who it is and describe a mood instead of a moment (outlier titles narrate the streamer's setup: 44 of 45 500K+ clip Shorts name the streamer first, 43 of 45 end on an emoji). Keep them only for a skit with no streamer in it: `clipper check` fails them when the description or source names a streamer. Name a second big streamer when one is in the clip. Vary the verbs across titles. The title goes with that hook's MP4. End on the emoji and never add a series tag after a pipe ("| GTA RP", "| NoPixel"): 71% of our titles had one vs 3% of 100K+ clip Shorts, and 81% of theirs end on an emoji vs 23% of ours (research/outlier-analysis-2026-11-20.md; a style gap, not a proven cause). Max 100 characters, 1–2 CAPS words, include "GTA 6"/"GTA VI" (a joke title may skip it if #gta6 is in the hashtags).
   - **Opening frame (stricter picking):** the first second must show the streamer's face reacting or something visibly happening in the game. Never open on driving, a dark room, a loading or black screen, or people standing around (our flops all did; research "Why our Shorts stall"). `clipper check --job` **fails** a dark opening and notes a static one: move `start` to a lit, moving moment or drop the clip. Post fewer, stronger clips: a clip with no visible action in its first second isn't worth the test audience.
   - **Start time:** `candidates.md` shows each candidate's opening line type. The viral clips opened in-scene, on action, a claim, a question or a number promise, never on filler. If it says filler, move `start` to the first strong line.
   - **Debate (one per clip when it honestly fits):** at least one hook hands the viewer a verdict on a real disagreement in the clip, so they rewatch and argue in the comments ("Is xQc's aim *legit*?!", "Was Kai *wrong* for this?", "Who won this *argument*?"). Research "Outlier study": "Is He Cheating?!" 7.8M (29x); verdict questions ~1.28x on 36 gaming titles. Never invent a dispute the clip doesn't show, and never insult (overrated/trash/ruined ~0.84x). `clipper check` notes a clip with no verdict hook.
   - **Emotion:** name each clip's strongest honest emotion (shock, anger, laughter, awe, sympathy, suspense) in the picks table, and write every hook and title to push that one emotion as hard as the clip backs up: sharper verbs, a CAPS word on the emotional beat, the matching emoji in the title (😳 shock, 😡 anger, 💀 laughter, 🤯 awe, 😭 sympathy). Overclaiming ("Kai CRIES" when he smirks) is the clickbait research rule 8 bans.
   - **Concrete stake:** every hook names what's at stake: a number or amount, a consequence, or a named character or thing that isn't the streamer ("Kai was NOT ready for *Trevor*", "him to be *13*", "Kai's *$500K* painting"). On our channel the top 3 Shorts all did; the bottom 4 named only a vague object ("What's on xQc's *USB*?", "a *marlin*", "the *betrayal*"). The 3,481-Short dataset doesn't show this effect in titles yet, so `clipper check` only notes it.
   - **Name anchor:** for a well-known streamer, make at least one hook a premise with their name and one CAPS twist word ("xQc forces the hostages to *SING*"); fans stop on the name.
   - **Vary the wording across videos:** when you make several videos, no two hooks or titles may open with the same words ("Kai was NOT ready for…" on every clip reads as templated), and where two hook types fit a clip about equally well, lead with one the other videos aren't leading with. A feed that repeats one formula gets swiped.
   - Then run `python3 -m clipper check jobs/<id>/picks.json --job <id>`. Then `python3 -m clipper simulate jobs/<id>/picks.json` and put the top-ranked hook first. Fix every **fail**; **notes** are judgment calls you may keep if you can say why.
   - **voiceover:** one line of 10 words or fewer, following the research's "When to add an AI voiceover".
